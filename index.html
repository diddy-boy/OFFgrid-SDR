Tune to a signal of known strength (a signal generator, or a beacon with a known level), set manual RF gain, type its level and press Calibrate. HF and VHF are stored separately. The S-meter needs manual gain, because AGC hides the real gain.
Bias-tee (antenna power) For LNAs and active antennas made to be powered this way (RTL-SDR Blog V3 and V4: 4.5 V). Leave it off otherwise: some antennas short DC to ground, and powering them can damage the stick or the antenna. The red BIAS light shows when it's on.
DC spike Removes the stick's constant offset, which shows as a spike in the middle of the MW and SW views.
RDS (FM) RDS needs a better signal than the audio does. On a weaker station the name may take a few seconds to build up (missing letters show as dots). If RDS drops out, the last name and text stay on screen, dimmed.
Peak limiter It only acts if what's coming out of your speakers or headphones would reach full scale, so at normal volume it leaves the programme alone.
CW decoder Shown bottom left in CW mode. Best on clean, steady signals such as beacons; it learns the speed by itself. The first letter or so of a transmission may be missed while it locks on.
Weather fax Each finished chart is saved as a PNG to your Downloads folder, so none are missed while you're away. (Even with this off, a chart isn't lost if the next one starts before you save it: use "Save previous" in the fax box.) Chrome may ask once to allow several downloads: choose Allow.

Saves all 50 memories, each band's last frequency and mode, filters, calibration and other settings to a small file, for example to keep on the USB stick with OFFgrid-SDR, or to copy them to another computer. Importing replaces the current settings.

OFFgrid-SDR

Offline RTL-SDR receiver for your browser

Quick help

  • Works with the RTL-SDR Blog V4 (and V3), and the Nooelec NESDR SMArt v5, both tested by the OFFgrid-SDR author. Buy the Nooelec: Amazon UK · Amazon US.
  • Antennas tested with OFFgrid-SDR: the AURSINC GA800 active loop shortwave antenna (Amazon UK · Amazon US), and the YouLoop passive magnetic loop, which folds up small for travel (Amazon UK · Amazon US).
  • Zoom (under Seek and Step, or the + and − keys): Zoom + narrows the waterfall around the tuned signal, down to a few kHz across, with the detail to match, handy for SSB and CW. Default goes back to the full view.
  • Any frequency the stick can reach (100 kHz to 1766 MHz) can be typed into the frequency display. Add k or M to be explicit, e.g. 198k or 1090M; a plain number below 500 is MHz, 500 and above is kHz. Frequencies outside the bands open in General coverage, with a moving window and all modes (AM, SAM, USB, LSB, CW, NFM, WFM).
  • Start: press Power on, pick your stick in the USB chooser, then choose a band.
  • Type a frequency: click the big frequency display (or just type digits) and press Enter. 98.8 → FM, 909 → MW, 6070 or 6.07 → the whole 49 m band.
  • Waterfall: click a signal to tune to it, or click anywhere else to tune there. Clicking close to the tuning line steps one channel left or right, for fine nudging. Shift-click tunes to the exact spot. In band views you can tune anywhere visible, including just outside the official band edges. Arrow keys and the mouse wheel stop at the edge of the view.
  • Seek stops on the first channel above the squelch level (dB of S/N).
  • Mode buttons under FM / MW / SW: Stereo / Wide / Mono on FM. On MW and SW: AM, SAM (synchronous, for fading), SAM-U / SAM-L (one sideband only, to dodge a neighbour), USB / LSB for SSB (2 kHz audio, 100 Hz steps), and CW for Morse (the carrier is heard as a 600 Hz tone; 100 to 1000 Hz filter on the bandwidth badge; 10 Hz steps).
  • Meter: click the scale button to switch between dBFS, S-meter and S/N. Calibrate the S-meter in Receiver settings.
  • Broadcast / Ham under the band buttons switches the grid between the shortwave broadcast metre bands and the amateur bands (160 to 10 m, plus 2 m on VHF, which runs the stick at 2.4 MS/s so the whole 144–146 MHz band fits on the waterfall, and 70 cm on UHF, 430–440 MHz, which is too wide for one view and tunes with a moving window). PMR446 (in the Ham tab) is the licence-free walkie-talkie band used in the UK and Europe: 16 NFM channels from 446.00625 to 446.19375 MHz, all shown at once. Step + / Step − and the mouse wheel move channel by channel, and the readout and banner show the channel number. Mystery collects famous unexplained HF stations: The Buzzer (UVB-76), The Pip, The Squeaky Wheel and other Russian military "channel markers". Each opens a narrow view on the station; they're mostly buzzes and beeps, with rare voice messages. Airband covers 118–137 MHz in 2 MHz sections, each shown whole, in AM with 8.33 kHz channel steps (25 kHz on the step badge). The display shows 8.33 kHz channel names, and typing one (e.g. 118.510) tunes that channel. Squelch (the SQ light) is handy there, as most channels are silent between calls. Each ham band starts in the conventional sideband (LSB below 10 MHz, USB above) and remembers its last frequency and mode.
  • Demo mode: open the page as index.html?demo to try everything without a stick. The Demo stations list tunes simulated stations: four FM stations (stereo and RDS), the 49 m band with a station for each filter (fading for SAM, mains hum, electric-fence clicks for the noise blanker, and a weak hissy station for noise reduction), and the 41 m band with SSB voice in LSB and USB, a CW Morse beacon and an AM broadcaster.
  • Identify ↗ (above the waterfall, when online): opens the Signal Identification Wiki in a new tab, either the list of known signals in the band you're tuned to, or its search form, with the frequency copied for pasting. Compare its waterfall pictures and audio samples with what you see and hear.
  • CW decoder: in CW mode, Morse is turned into text in a box at the bottom left, with the speed in WPM. It learns the sender's speed by itself and stays blank on noise. It works best on clean, steady signals such as beacons (switch it off in Receiver settings).
  • Settings backup (Receiver settings): Export saves all memories and settings to a small file, for example to keep on the USB stick; Import restores them, on this or another computer.
  • FAX (weather fax, shortwave): choose a station from the list in the fax box, or tune a listed frequency and pick FAX mode. Charts are sent at scheduled times: the decoder waits for each chart's start tone, lines it up automatically and builds the picture line by line (about 10 minutes per chart at 120 lines a minute). "Start now" begins mid-chart; then click the chart where its left edge should be. Save PNG keeps the full-resolution chart.
  • RDS on FM: the station name, RadioText, programme type and clock appear in a box at the bottom left of the waterfall once they're decoded. FM memories and recordings pick up the station name.
  • REC (or R) records what you hear. Press again to stop, and the recording downloads as MP3 (or WAV, in Receiver settings).
  • Noise filters (MW/SW, Audio & gain): Reduction for hiss, Hum 50/60 Hz for mains buzz, Noise blanker for clicks and pops.
  • Keys: ← → step, Shift + ← → seek, M mute, ? this panel.
